Both look sleek, both lose the shower door and both make a bathroom feel bigger. But a wet room and a walk-in shower are built in very different ways, and the right choice depends on your floor, your budget and who will use the room. Here is how they compare.

## What’s the difference?

A **walk-in shower** uses a low-profile shower tray, usually 25 to 45mm high, with one or two fixed glass panels and no door. You step over a small lip into the shower area.

A **wet room** has no tray at all. The floor itself is waterproofed (tanked) and gently sloped towards a drain, so the shower area is level with the rest of the room. Often the entire room is waterproofed.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Feature | Walk-in shower | Wet room |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access | Small step (25–45mm) | Completely level |
| Typical cost (bathroom) | £9,000 – £13,000 | £10,000 – £19,000 |
| Installation time | 1–2 weeks | 2–3 weeks |
| Floor work | Minimal | Floor lowered or former fitted; full tanking |
| Best for | Most bathrooms, en-suites, tighter budgets | Accessibility, small rooms, a seamless look |
| Cleaning | Easy; tray edge to wipe | Easiest; no tray, drain lifts out |
| Risk if badly built | Leaks at tray seal | Leaks through floor; poor drainage |

## When a walk-in shower makes more sense

- **You want a quick, cost-effective upgrade.** A walk-in shower doesn’t usually need the floor lowering, so it is faster and cheaper.
- **Your floor makes a wet room difficult.** On some upstairs floors, the joists run the wrong way for the waste to reach the soil stack at the right fall.
- **There’s a small step you can live with.** For most people, a 25mm tray is not a barrier.
- **You still want a bath.** A walk-in shower plus a bath fits in many family bathrooms; a full wet room usually doesn’t.

## When a wet room is the better choice

- **Accessibility matters now or in the future.** Level access means no step to trip on and room for a shower chair or wheelchair. See our page on [accessible bathrooms](/accessible-bathrooms).
- **The room is small.** In a compact en-suite, removing the tray and treating the whole floor as the shower can make the room feel much larger.
- **You want a seamless, contemporary look.** The same tile running uninterrupted from the door to the wall is hard to beat.
- **You hate cleaning tray edges.** With no tray, there are no seals to go mouldy.

## What about resale value?

A well-built wet room is a selling point, especially as an en-suite or a second bathroom. If it is your only bathroom, many buyers with young children will still want a bath somewhere in the house. A walk-in shower alongside a bath, or a wet room en-suite with a bath in the family bathroom, keeps the widest appeal.

## The question that matters most: who builds it?

Whichever you choose, the quality of the installation is what you’ll live with. For walk-in showers, the tray must be perfectly level and supported, with a sealed waste and properly tanked walls. For wet rooms, the floor must fall correctly to the drain and be fully waterproofed and flood-tested before tiling.
