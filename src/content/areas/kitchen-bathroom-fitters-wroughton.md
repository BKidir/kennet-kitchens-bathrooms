Wroughton sits on the edge of the downs just south of Swindon, close enough to town for an easy commute, but with a proper village feel. Its housing is a mix of older stone and brick cottages around the centre, large numbers of 1960s and 1970s family homes, and some newer infill developments. We have worked on all of them.

## Typical Wroughton homes and what they need

**1960s and 1970s semis and detached houses** make up much of the village. Their bathrooms are often original or were last updated in the 1990s, with small rooms, coloured suites and electric showers over the bath. These are ideal for a full [bathroom installation](/bathroom-installation): a fresh layout, a proper thermostatic shower and better storage. One thing we check on every job here is the floor under the old bath, because decades of small leaks can leave joists in need of repair.

**Older cottages** along the High Street and in Overtown have thicker walls, low ceilings and more character. Kitchens in these homes benefit from careful design: in-frame or Shaker styles, solid wood worktops and storage that works around beams and alcoves.

**Bungalows** are common on the edges of the village, and we fit many walk-in showers and [accessible bathrooms](/accessible-bathrooms) in them for homeowners who want to stay put for the long term.

**Family homes with no downstairs toilet** are another regular request. A [cloakroom under the stairs](/cloakrooms) is one of the most useful upgrades you can make to a busy household.

## A recent Wroughton project

Mark and Sophie’s 1970s semi still had its original bathroom, and four people shared one shower over the bath every morning. We moved the shower to the internal wall, put a freestanding bath under the window and added a double vanity. When we lifted the floor we found two rotten joists, so we showed them photos, agreed the repair price the same day and fixed it properly before laying underfloor heating and a herringbone tiled floor. The job finished on schedule in twelve working days.
