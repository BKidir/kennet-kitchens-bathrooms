Cirencester, the old Roman town of Corinium, is often called the capital of the Cotswolds. Its homes are built from the warm local limestone, from medieval and Georgian buildings in the centre to Victorian terraces in Watermoor and newer estates such as Kingshill. It’s a pleasure to work here.

## Cirencester homes and their quirks

**Stone cottages and period townhouses** in the centre and surrounding villages have thick walls that are rarely straight, deep window reveals and, often, listed status. Kitchens and bathrooms here need to be designed to the building. That means scribed panels, templated stone worktops and fittings chosen to complement the stone.

**Victorian terraces** in Watermoor and around the town centre have similar layouts to those we work on in Swindon: a back kitchen, a separate dining room and a small bathroom often added later at the back of the house. Opening up the kitchen and making better use of the bathroom are popular projects.

**Mid-century and later homes** in Chesterton, Stratton and The Beeches tend to be easier to work in, with more regular rooms that suit islands and walk-in showers.

**New-build homes** at Kingshill and on the edges of the town often have small en-suites that customers want to improve with walk-in showers and better storage.

## A Cirencester en-suite

In a stone cottage just outside the town, the owners wanted an en-suite in what had been a box room. The space had a sloping ceiling and an exposed beam running across it. We positioned the walk-in shower where the headroom was greatest, built a vanity unit around the beam, and used limestone-effect porcelain tiles that echo the stone outside without the maintenance of real limestone. The room was finished in eight working days.

## Listed homes and the National Landscape

Much of the area around Cirencester lies within the Cotswolds National Landscape, and many homes are listed. Internal work is often straightforward, but some changes need consent. We’ll talk this through at the design visit and adjust the design where needed.
