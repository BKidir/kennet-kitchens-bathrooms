Swindon’s homes tell the story of the town. Railway cottages built for GWR workers, Victorian terraces climbing up to Old Town, post-war estates, the 1970s and 80s expansion of West Swindon, and the newer communities at Wichelstowe and Tadpole. Each brings its own quirks, and after more than a decade fitting kitchens and bathrooms here, we know most of them.

## The homes we work on across Swindon

**Victorian and Edwardian terraces** in Old Town, Rodbourne and Gorse Hill are where we do many of our kitchen knock-throughs. The typical layout is a narrow galley kitchen behind a separate dining room, and opening them into one space transforms how the house works. These homes come with uneven walls, suspended timber floors and occasional surprises like lead pipework, so we survey thoroughly before quoting.

**The Railway Village** has some of the most characterful homes in town. Many are listed, with small rooms and solid walls, so we design compact kitchens and bathrooms that respect the original features and check whether consent is needed before we begin.

**1960s to 1980s homes** in Covingham, Park North, Freshbrook, Shaw and Westlea tend to have practical layouts but tired original bathrooms and kitchens. Bathrooms here are often small, with a window in the wrong place for a shower, which is where good design earns its keep.

**1990s and 2000s estates** such as Abbey Meads, Haydon Wick and Priory Vale often have kitchens that are structurally fine but dated. These are perfect for our [kitchen refurbishment](/kitchen-refurbishment) service: new doors, worktops and lighting for a fraction of the price of a new kitchen.

**New builds** at Wichelstowe, Tadpole Garden Village and Redhouse usually come with builder-standard kitchens and small en-suites. We regularly upgrade both, often within the first few years of ownership.
