Most en-suites we see across Swindon fall into one of two groups. The first is a builder-standard new-build en-suite with a narrow quadrant shower, a pedestal basin and very little storage. The second is an older conversion, squeezed into a corner of a 1960s or 1970s bedroom, with low water pressure and a fan that hums but doesn’t clear the steam. Both can become something far better.

## Designing a small en-suite

The design stage is where most of the space is won. A few of the techniques we use regularly:

- **Walk-in showers instead of enclosures.** A single fixed glass panel uses less floor space than a quadrant door swing and makes the room look twice the size.
- **Wall-hung everything.** A floating vanity and wall-hung toilet with a concealed cistern show more floor and are easier to clean.
- **Recessed storage.** Niches in the shower and a recessed mirror cabinet keep things off the surfaces without taking up floor space.
- **Pocket or sliding doors.** These free up the wall the door used to swing against.
- **Short-projection toilets and slim basins.** Saving 10cm in front of the toilet can make the difference between a cramped room and a comfortable one.
- **Light, layered lighting.** Downlights, an illuminated mirror and a low-level night light change how a small room feels.

We show you each option in 3D, so you can see what you gain.

## Adding a new en-suite

If you don’t have an en-suite yet, we can create one. We look at:

1. **Where the space comes from**: part of the bedroom, a box room or a built-in wardrobe.
2. **Drainage**: the toilet ideally needs to be close to the soil stack. Where it can’t be, a quality macerator is a reliable option.
3. **Water supply and pressure**: especially if you have a combi boiler serving several showers.
4. **Ventilation**: ducting a fan to the outside, through a wall or the loft.
5. **Building regulations**: we handle notifications for new drainage and electrics.

We build the stud walls, insulate them for sound, board, tank and tile, then fit everything out. You deal with one team throughout.

## Cottages, conversions and character homes

En-suites in older properties around Cirencester, Marlborough and the villages bring extra challenges: sloping ceilings, uneven floors, thick stone walls and listed building considerations. We have fitted en-suites under eaves, between exposed beams and in converted barns, using tiles and fittings that respect the character of the house rather than fighting it.

## A small room, done properly

Small rooms show every imperfection. A tile cut that doesn’t line up, a silicone bead that wanders, a gap behind the basin. Our own [tiling](/tiling) team takes the time to set out each wall so cuts land in the right places, and we guarantee the workmanship for ten years.
